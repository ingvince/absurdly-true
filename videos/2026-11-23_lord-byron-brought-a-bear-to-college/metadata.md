# Dogs Were Banned, So Lord Byron Got a Pet Bear — 2026-11-23

## Facts (verified)
- Lord Byron studied at Trinity College, Cambridge (from 1805). Dogs were not allowed in college; he kept a tame bear instead.
- Letter to Elizabeth Pigot, 26 Oct 1807: "I have got a new friend, the finest in the world, a tame bear. When I brought him here,
  they asked me what I meant to do with him, and my reply was, 'he should sit for a fellowship.'"
- Walking it on a chain around the grounds and "the rules said nothing about bears" = popular accounts (framed as such in the description).

## Script (98 words, MiniMax "Decent Young Man", 36.57 s, 1097 frames)
In 1807, a student brought a pet bear to Cambridge University. On purpose. His name was Lord Byron, and his college, Trinity,
had a strict rule: no dogs allowed. Byron loved his dog. But the rules never said anything about bears. So he got a tame bear,
moved it into his rooms, and walked it around the college on a chain, like a puppy. Shocked, the college asked what he meant to
do with it. Byron had his answer ready. The bear, he said, should sit for a fellowship. In other words... the bear should become a teacher.
