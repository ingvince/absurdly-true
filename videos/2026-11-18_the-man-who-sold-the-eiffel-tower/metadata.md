# The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower… Twice — 42.8 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps

Facts (verified; details come from later accounts of Lustig's life): in 1925 a Paris newspaper reported the Eiffel Tower
needed costly repairs (the city had even considered tearing it down). Con man "Count" Victor Lustig forged government
stationery, posed as Deputy Director General of the Ministère des Postes et Télégraphes, and invited five leading scrap
iron dealers to a confidential meeting at a top hotel (Hôtel de Crillon in most accounts). André Poisson "bought" the
tower and also paid Lustig a bribe. Lustig fled to Austria; Poisson was too embarrassed to go to the police. About six
months later Lustig returned and ran the scheme again; that victim went to the police and Lustig fled to the USA.

A con man once sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice. Paris, nineteen twenty-five. A newspaper says the tower is rusting, and
repairs will cost a fortune. Victor Lustig reads it, and gets an idea. He forges government letters, poses as an
official, and invites five scrap metal dealers to a secret meeting at a fancy hotel. The tower, he says, will be sold
for scrap. One dealer, André Poisson, buys it. He even slips him a bribe. Lustig flees to Austria with the money. But
Poisson is too embarrassed to tell the police. So, six months later, Lustig comes back, and sells it again.
