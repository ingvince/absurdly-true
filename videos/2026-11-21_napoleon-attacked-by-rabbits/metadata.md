# Napoleon Was Attacked by Hundreds of Rabbits — 37.0 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps

Facts (verified; the anecdote comes from the memoirs of French general Paul Thiébault, details vary between retellings):
in July 1807, after the Treaties of Tilsit, Napoleon's chief of staff Marshal Louis-Alexandre Berthier organised a
celebratory rabbit hunt. Hundreds of rabbits (some accounts say up to 3,000) were gathered in cages. When released they
did not flee but charged at Napoleon, swarming his feet and legs; whips and shots did not scare them off and the emperor
retreated to his carriage. Berthier's men had bought tame rabbits from farmers instead of catching wild ones, so the
rabbits expected food.
