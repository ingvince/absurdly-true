# Why the Leaning Tower of Pisa Never Fell — 39.0 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps

Facts (verified): foundations laid August 1173; the tower began to lean after construction reached the second floor in 1178,
on a 3 m foundation in weak, unstable subsoil. Work was halted for most of a century because Pisa was almost constantly at
war with Genoa, Lucca and Florence (resumed 1272); the pause let the underlying soil settle — "otherwise, the tower would
almost certainly have toppled" (Wikipedia). Engineers then built upper floors with one side taller than the other, so the
tower is curved. Bell chamber added 1372. Tilt 5.5° by 1990; closed 1990-2001; ~70 t of soil removed, now ~3.97°.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Pisa · https://www.ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/what-do-civil-engineers-do/stabilising-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa/

## Script (voice: MiniMax "Decent Young Man", speech-2.8-hd)
The Leaning Tower of Pisa only survived because of war.
In eleven seventy-three, Pisa began a proud new bell tower.
Five years later, at the second floor, it started to sink.
The foundation was just three meters deep, in soft mud.
Then Pisa went to war with Genoa, Lucca and Florence.
Work stopped for almost a century.
And that saved it. The soil slowly settled under the weight.
Experts say without the pause, it would almost certainly have toppled.
When builders finally came back, they made each new floor taller on one side.
So the tower isn't just leaning. It's bent.
Peace would have knocked it down.
