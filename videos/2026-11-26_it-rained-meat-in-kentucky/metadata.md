# It Rained Meat in Kentucky — 2026-11-26

## Facts (verified) + sources
- 3 March 1876, ~11 a.m.–noon, a 100 × 50 yard area near Olympia Springs, Bath County, Kentucky: flakes of meat fell for several minutes.
- Mrs. Crouch was making soap on her porch; she said it was "snowing meat". Most flakes ~2 × 2 in, at least one ~4 × 4 in.
- Two men tasted it and judged it possibly lamb (mutton) or deer (venison).
- Theories: Leopold Brandeis → Nostoc (cyanobacteria); Allan McLane Hamilton → lung tissue (horse or human infant).
- Dr. L. D. Kastenbine (Louisville Medical News, 1876) → vultures disgorging; "seeing one of their companions disgorge himself, immediately followed suit". Scientific American (2014) calls it the most plausible explanation.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_meat_shower · https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/running-ponies/the-great-kentucky-meat-shower-mystery-unwound-by-projectile-vulture-vomit/ · https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/59487/time-it-rained-flesh-kentucky

Flakes up to four inches wide covered a field a hundred yards long. She said it was snowing meat. Then two brave neighbors did what anyone would do. They tasted it. Verdict? Lamb. Or maybe deer. Scientists couldn't agree. The best explanation? A flock of vultures overhead. When one vulture throws up, the others follow. So the meat from the sky? Vulture vomit.
