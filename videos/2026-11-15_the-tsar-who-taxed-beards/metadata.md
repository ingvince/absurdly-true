# The Tsar Who Taxed Beards — 36.1 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps

Facts (verified): Peter the Great returned in 1698 from the Grand Embassy (1697-98) to Western Europe and personally shaved
the beards of senior boyars at a reception/ceremonial event in front of everyone. In September 1698 he introduced the beard
tax: rich merchants 100 rubles/year, other merchants/townsfolk 60, Muscovites 30, peasants two half-kopeks per city entry.
Payers carried a copper/silver "beard token" (1699/1705 issues read "ДЕНГИ ВЗЯТЫ" = "money taken"; the 1724 issue's edge reads
"the beard is a superfluous burden"). Police could forcibly shave men who refused to pay. Repealed by Catherine the Great in
1772 (74 years). Peter himself wore a moustache.

Russia once made men pay to keep their beards.
In sixteen ninety-eight, Tsar Peter the Great came home from Europe, where men shaved.

The tax lasted seventy-four years.
