# The Bath That Caught a Cheat — 40.2 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps

Facts (legend, framed as "Legend says"): the story comes from Vitruvius, De Architectura, Book IX (1st century BC,
~200 years after Archimedes). King Hiero II of Syracuse ordered a gold crown (votive wreath) and suspected the
contractor had replaced some gold with silver. Archimedes, asked to check it without damaging it, noticed the water
overflowing as he got into a full bath, leapt out and ran home naked shouting "Eureka!" ("I have found it!"). Equal
weights of gold and silver displace different volumes of water; the crown displaced more than the same weight of pure
gold, proving the fraud. Historians (and Galileo) doubt the exact method.

## Script (voice: MiniMax "Decent Young Man", speech-2.8-hd, 40.15 s)
A man ran naked through the streets, because of a bathtub. Legend says that in ancient Syracuse, King Hiero ordered a
crown of pure gold. But he suspected the goldsmith had swapped some of the gold for cheap silver. So he asked the genius
Archimedes to prove it, without melting the crown. Archimedes was stuck. Until he stepped into a full bath, and the water
spilled over. Silver is lighter than gold, so the same weight takes up more space, and pushes out more water. He leapt
out and ran home naked, shouting, "Eureka!" The crown spilled more water than pure gold. The cheat was caught. By a bath.
